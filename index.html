How this is calculated
  • Everything is in today's dollars, so returns are after inflation.
  • Markets. Strong: stocks 6%, homes 1.5% a year. Base: 4.5% and 1%. Weak: 2.5% and flat. Crash next year: stocks fall 30% and homes 10% next year, then base returns with no rebound.
  • Investments earn a blend of the stock return and 1.5% for bonds, by the mix you pick. All accounts are treated as one pool, including retirement accounts, so early-withdrawal penalties aren't modeled.
  • Career paths are take-home pay, after tax, until your retirement age. Social Security starts at the age you set.
  • Home. The home grows at the market's home rate. The mortgage is paid down from your monthly principal and interest; tax and insurance continue for life. Net worth counts home value minus what you owe. The home is kept, not sold.
  • Spending continues at the same level for life. Each year, income minus spending, housing costs and mortgage payments is added to or drawn from investments.
  • Retirement income is shown as 4% of investments at retirement plus Social Security.
  • Taxes on investment growth and withdrawals are ignored. A single stock is modeled like the overall market, though it can swing far wider in either direction.
  • These are rough projections for thinking, not financial advice. A fee-only planner can stress-test your real plan.
